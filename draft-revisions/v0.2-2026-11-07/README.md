# AI Social Publishing

Plan a series of posts around subjects your audience cares about, and get approval on the exact version before anything goes out.

This skill is Muse first and Meta first. Muse drafts the posts, you approve them, and Muse publishes to Facebook Pages, Instagram professional accounts, and Threads. That is the core path, and it is the only path you need to start.

It works for a person or for a company. Start with one post if you want a trial.

## Start with a brief

Give Muse the [skill folder](skills/social-publishing/) and adapt this request.

```text
Plan three posts for [audience] about [industry and topics].
Use [brand notes or sample posts] if supplied.
Propose dates and times for [channels] in [time zone].
Use my own photos or video first. Use a public domain image only if I have none that fits.
Show me the posts, images, sources, credits, and schedule for review.
```

Then answer the [intake questions](skills/social-publishing/templates/first-use.md). They cover who you are, who the posts are for, what they should do, how you sound, and how often you want to post. There is no fixed day or schedule in this skill. You set the schedule in intake, and you can change it later.

A vintage computing shop, for example, could ask for three stories about early personal computers. Muse would use the shop's own photos first, look for a public domain image only if the shop has none that fits, check the rights for that file, and show a batch to review. A batch is a group of posts drafted together for your review.

## How it works

1. Answer the intake questions. Tell Muse who the posts are for, what they should do, and how often you want to post.
2. Muse drafts a batch. Muse finds stories that fit your brief, uses your own photos and video first, and writes a version for each channel.
3. You approve the exact version. Muse shows you the copy, the photo or video, the credit, the account, and the time for each post. Nothing is scheduled or published until you approve that exact version.
4. Muse posts it and records it. Muse submits only approved posts to their verified accounts, then records the result in your private post history.

If copy, media, account, or timing changes, the post goes back for approval. That rule does not change.

## What you need

You need a Facebook Page, an Instagram professional account, or a Threads account. Muse connects to those accounts directly. See [Muse First](skills/social-publishing/integrations/muse-first.md).

You need your own photos or video. That comes first. See the [image guide](skills/social-publishing/references/images.md). It explains why your own files come first, and how a public domain file is checked when it is needed.

You do not need a scheduler, a dashboard, or another app to start.

## Optional add-ons

It lists LinkedIn, TikTok Reels, Composio, Buffer, manual publishing, and using this skill with another AI. You do not need any of them to start. Add one only when you want it.

## Using this skill with another AI

The skill is plain text, so another AI can read it. It is not tested outside Muse, and there are no guarantees there. Read [Adapting to Another AI](skills/social-publishing/integrations/optional/adapting-to-other-ai.md) in the appendix before you try it. Do not assume another AI has the same connections, approvals, or publishing checks as Muse.

## What this skill does not do

It does not post without your approval. It does not invent facts, dates, prices, or quotes. It does not use a photo or video without a clear source and permission. It does not set a schedule or a recurring run without your specific approval.

The workflow includes a [fictional draft](skills/social-publishing/examples/fictional-post.json). The existing tests cover the offline example. Publishing depends on the tools and permissions available for your accounts.
