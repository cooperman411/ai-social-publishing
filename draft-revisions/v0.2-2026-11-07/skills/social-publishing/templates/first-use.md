# Set Up a Publishing Brief

This is the intake interview. Muse asks the questions in order, one section at a time. Answer what you can. Skip a question, answer it approximately, or mark it To confirm. Muse never blocks on an optional answer, and Muse never invents a value for a missing answer.

Start with one draft post when you want to test the process.

## 1. Personal or company

These questions come first. They change the voice, the accounts, and who approves.

1. Is this for you personally, or for a company or organization?
2. If it is a company, what is the company name? What does it do? Who speaks in the posts? Name the founder, the team, or the brand.
3. If it is personal, what do you want to be known for? What do you not want to post about?
4. Who approves the posts? Is that you, or someone else? If someone else, give their name and role, and say how they approve.
5. Who else can supply facts, photos, or video? Who can confirm a price, date, or claim before it goes out?

## 2. Audience and goal

1. Who should read the posts? Name them in plain words. Examples: local customers, past guests, hobbyists, or other business owners.
2. Where are they? Give a town, region, or time zone if it matters.
3. What should the posts do? Pick one main goal. Examples: bring in bookings, build trust, teach something useful, or stay in touch with past customers.
4. What is one clear next step for a reader? Examples: visit a page, call, book, or reply. Keep it to one per post.
5. What should Muse never claim, promise, or post about?

## 3. Topics and facts

1. What topics can Muse use? List 3 to 5 to start.
2. What topics are off limits?
3. What facts, links, offers, prices, or dates can Muse use? Give the source for each. Examples: your website, a price list, or a booking page.
4. Are there seasonal topics, launches, holidays, or local events to plan around?
5. How should Muse handle a fact it cannot verify? The default is to leave that slot open, or to use a verified owned photo with no claim.

## 4. Voice

1. Give Muse a voice guide or sample posts if you have them. If you do not have them, describe the tone in a few words.
2. How formal should it sound? Plain and direct is the default. See [Editorial](../references/editorial.md).
3. Are there words, phrases, or hashtags to use, or to avoid?
4. How should links and hashtags work for you? Examples: link in bio on Instagram, a full link on Facebook, or no hashtags at all.

## 5. Channels and accounts

The core channels are Facebook Page, Instagram professional account, and Threads, in Muse.

1. Which core channels do you want? What is the exact account for each? Muse verifies each account before drafting for it.
2. Do you want a personal profile post as well as a Page post, or Page only? Name the account for each.
3. Do you want any optional add-ons now, or later? Read [APPENDIX.md](../../../APPENDIX.md) on the repo home page. LinkedIn and TikTok Reels are the usual ones. Say no for now if you are not sure. You can add an add-on later without changing the core brief.
4. If you want video or Reels, do you have vertical video already? Or do you want to start with photos and add video later?

## 6. Cadence: frequency, days, and times

Cadence means how often you post, and on which days and at what times. There is no fixed cadence in this skill. Tell Muse what you want, then use the suggestions below as starting points. A rhythm you can keep beats a busy rhythm that stops after two weeks.

Ask:

1. How often do you want to post? Posts per week is the usual unit.
2. Which days work for you to review a batch? Which days should posts go out?
3. What times work for your audience, in their time zone? When are they awake and likely to see it?
4. Do you want Muse to draft a whole batch at once for your review, or draft one post at a time?
5. What is the review deadline for each batch? When does Muse need your approval by?
6. Are there dates to avoid? Examples: closures, holidays, or busy weeks.
7. Do you want scheduled publishing, a manual posting list, or drafts only for now?
8. What should happen when you pause? Who says pause, and what happens to posts that are already scheduled?

### Suggestions to offer

Offer these as starting points only. They are not rules. Let the person pick, change, or ignore them. Check the account's own insights later, and use what the audience actually does over generic best times.

- Start small. Post 1 time to test the process, then post 2 to 3 times per week when the rhythm holds.
- Pick the same days each week, so review becomes a habit. For example, draft and review on one day, then post on 2 or 3 set days.
- Post when your audience is awake and free to read. Do not post at midnight in their time zone. Midday and early evening are common starting points for a local audience. Morning can work for a work audience.
- Leave at least a day between posts at first, so you can see what lands and fix what does not.
- Plan around real events, not around a calendar you cannot keep. If a week is busy, post less that week and say so in the brief.
- For a personal account, 1 to 2 posts a week is a good start. For a small company with photos and news to share, 2 to 3 posts a week is a good start. More than 5 a week is rarely sustainable at first.

Record the agreed cadence in the brief: posts per week, days, times, time zone, review day and deadline, and pause rule.

## 7. Photos and video

Your own photos and video come first. See [Image Search and Rights](../references/images.md).

1. Do you have your own photos or video to use? Where are they, and who took them?
2. Can Muse use customer or community photos or video? Muse uses them only with clear permission from the person who took them, and with credit as agreed.
3. If you have no owned file that fits a story, may Muse look for a public domain or CC0 image? That is a fallback only, and Muse checks the exact file's rights before using it.
4. What credit should Muse use for your own photos? Examples: your name, your company, or no credit.
5. Are there people, places, or products that must not appear in a photo or video?

## 8. Where to keep it

1. Where should Muse keep the brief, calendar, files, and post history? Keep them in private storage, outside this public repo.
2. Do you want to reuse this brief for the next batch, or start fresh each time?
3. Is there anything else Muse should know before the first draft?

## After intake

Muse reads the answers back as a short brief: personal or company, audience, goal, topics, voice, channels and verified accounts, cadence, media rule, approver, and review deadline. Correct it, then Muse drafts the first batch.
