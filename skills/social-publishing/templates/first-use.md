# Set Up a Publishing Brief

This is the intake interview. Muse asks the questions in order, one section at a time. Answer what you can. Skip a question, answer it approximately, or mark it To confirm. Muse never blocks on an optional answer, and Muse never invents a value for a missing answer.

Start with one draft post when you want to test the process.

## 1. Purpose

1. Is this for you personally, or for a company or organization?
2. If it is a company, what is the company name? What does it do? Who speaks in the posts?
3. If it is personal, what do you want to be known for? What do you not want to post about?
4. Who approves the posts? Give their name and role, and say how they approve.
5. Who else can supply facts, photos, or video?

## 2. Audience and goal

1. Who should read the posts? Name them in plain words.
2. Where are they? Give a town, region, or time zone if it matters.
3. What should the posts do? Pick one main goal.
4. What is one clear next step for a reader? Keep it to one per post.
5. What should Muse never claim, promise, or post about?

## 3. Topics and facts

1. What topics can Muse use? List 3 to 5 to start.
2. What topics are off limits?
3. What facts, links, offers, prices, or dates can Muse use? Give the source for each.
4. Are there seasonal topics, launches, holidays, or local events to plan around?

## 4. Voice

1. Give Muse a voice guide or sample posts if you have them. If you do not have them, describe the tone in a few words.
2. Are there words, phrases, or hashtags to use, or to avoid?
3. How should links and hashtags work for you? Examples: link in bio on Instagram, a full link on Facebook, or no hashtags at all.

## 5. Accounts

The core channels are Facebook Page, Instagram professional account, and Threads, in Muse.

1. Which core channels do you want? What is the exact account for each? Muse verifies each account before drafting for it.
2. Do you want a personal post as well as a company post, or company only? Say whether personal cross-posting is occasional or always, and name the account for each. Personal Facebook is text only in Muse. A personal photo or video needs the browser route or manual posting. Instagram posts to the selected account, personal or professional, and professional is the supported path. Threads accounts are personal style profiles tied to Instagram.
3. Do you want photos, short videos, or a mix?

## 6. Schedule

You decide how often to post. Muse records what you choose. One time a week is fine. Seven times a week is fine. There is no fixed schedule in this skill.

1. How often do you want to post? Posts per week is the usual unit.
2. Which days should posts go out? What times work for your audience, in their time zone?
3. Do you want Muse to draft a whole batch at once for your review, or draft one post at a time?
4. What is the review deadline for each batch?
5. Are there dates to avoid? Examples: closures, holidays, or busy weeks.
6. Do you want scheduled publishing, a manual posting list, or drafts only for now?
7. What should happen when you pause? Who says pause, and what happens to posts that are already scheduled?

### Starting points

Offer these as starting points only. They are not rules. Let the person pick, change, or ignore them.

- Start small. Post 1 time to test the process, then post 2 to 3 times per week when the rhythm holds.
- Pick the same days each week, so review becomes a habit.
- Post when your audience is awake and free to read. Midday and early evening are common starting points for a local audience.
- Leave at least a day between posts at first, so you can see what lands and fix what does not.

Record the agreed schedule in the brief: posts per week, days, times, time zone, review day and deadline, and pause rule.

## 7. Photos and video

Your own photos and video come first. See [Photos and Video](../references/images.md).

1. Do you have your own photos or video to use? Where are they? Attach them here, point Muse at a folder on your computer, name a Google Drive folder, use photos on your phone, or share a link to a Google Photos or Amazon Photos album. There is no direct connector for Google Photos or Amazon Photos, so a shared album link or a download is the route for those two.
2. Or do you want Muse to find photos for you? Or a mix, your photos when they fit and Muse finds one when they do not?
3. Can Muse use customer or community photos or video? Muse uses them only with clear permission from the person who took them, and with credit as agreed.
4. If you have no owned file that fits a story, may Muse look for a public domain or CC0 image? That is a fallback only, and Muse checks the exact file's rights before using it.
5. What credit should Muse use for your own photos? Examples: your name, your company, or no credit.
6. Are there people, places, or products that must not appear in a photo or video?

## 8. Where to keep it

1. Where should Muse keep the brief, calendar, files, and post history? Keep them in private storage, outside this public repo.
2. Do you want to reuse this brief for the next batch, or start fresh each time?
3. Is there anything else Muse should know before the first draft?

## After intake

Muse reads the answers back as a short brief: personal or company, audience, goal, topics, voice, channels and verified accounts, schedule, media rule, approver, and review deadline. Correct it, then Muse drafts the first batch.
