# Find and Verify Photos and Video

Use the photos and video you own when they fit the story. Use a real photo first. Use an AI image only when the person asks for one.

## Where your photos live

Ask the person where their photos live, and use that source first.

- Attach photos here, or upload a folder.
- Point Muse at a folder on the computer.
- Name a Google Drive folder.
- Use photos on the phone, through the connected device gallery.
- Share a link to a Google Photos or Amazon Photos album. There is no direct connector for Google Photos or Amazon Photos, so a shared album link or a download is the route for those two.

If the person has no owned file that fits, ask whether they want Muse to find one, or a mix: their photos when they fit, and Muse finds one when they do not.

Ask before using material from a customer, photographer, or other creator. Record the permission and the credit they asked for. Use stock only when the person asks for it and the license covers the post.

## Order to use

1. **Your own photos and video.** Use files the person made, or files made for the person with clear permission to publish. Check that the file shows what the caption says it shows.
2. **Customer or community photos and video.** Use only with clear permission from the person who took the file, and with credit as agreed. Record who gave permission, when, and for what use.
3. **Public domain or CC0, as a fallback only.** Use only when no owned file fits. Check the exact file's rights, as below. Do not start here.
4. **Free stock, only when the person asks for it.** Use only a license that covers this use, and record the license, source, and credit.

If no suitable file qualifies, leave the slot open, or use a verified owned photo with no claim. Keep other affected destinations in draft.

## Public domain fallback: where to search

Start with the subject, location, and era in the story. Search the organization, agency, museum, library, or archive most likely to hold the original image.

| Subject | Where to Start |
| --- | --- |
| Any subject, one search across sources | [Openverse](https://openverse.org/). Search over 800 million Creative Commons licensed and public domain images. Filter by license and commercial use. It gives a one-click attribution to copy. Open the source page and check the license before you publish. |
| Historic images from old books | [Internet Archive Book Images](https://www.flickr.com/photos/internetarchivebookimages/). About 14 million images from books published 1500 to 1922, public domain by age. Check the item page. |
| Space missions or astronomy | [NASA Image and Video Library](https://images.nasa.gov/) and [NASA media guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/). NASA images are US government work, generally public domain. Credit NASA. Do not imply NASA endorses the post. |
| Coasts, ocean science, or storms | [NOAA image collections](https://response.restoration.noaa.gov/resources/images). US government work, generally public domain. |
| Technology, artifacts, art, or natural history | [Smithsonian Open Access](https://www.si.edu/openaccess). Look for the CC0 mark on the actual media. |
| Historic places, people, buildings, or events | [Library of Congress Free to Use and Reuse](https://www.loc.gov/free-to-use/) or a local archive with item-level rights statements. |
| A subject without an obvious archive | [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia/licenses/en). Check the file page and trace the original source where possible. |
| Photos with no known copyright restrictions | [Flickr Commons](https://www.flickr.com/commons). Images from cultural institutions, separate from regular Flickr. |
| Modern real photos, when no historic file fits | [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/license), [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/license/), or [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/service/license/). Free to use commercially, no attribution required, but credit the photographer when the person wants it. Do not sell an unaltered photo, and do not imply a person in the photo endorses the product. These are free licenses, not public domain. |

The Internet Archive is a large source, and not everything there is public domain. Rights are whatever the uploader stated on that item page. Many items are non-commercial, or have no rights note. Treat no note as not cleared.

## Check the exact file

1. Open the item page. Check the creator, subject, date, and rights statement for the media itself. Prefer a documented public domain designation or CC0 dedication that fits the intended use. A site-wide label, image search filter, or CC0 metadata record alone does not establish the image's rights.
2. Check for third-party credits or additional restrictions relevant to the use. Pay particular attention to recognizable people, logos, and any suggestion of endorsement in a promotional post. Leave the item unresolved when you cannot establish permission.
3. Download a suitable file from the item page. Open it and confirm it shows the subject and era the caption describes. Label a representative image as such.
4. Save the original and any crop privately. Record the item URL, creator, rights statement and link, restrictions, exact credit, date checked, and the file used in the draft.
5. Show the image or video and credit alongside the caption. If no suitable file qualifies, offer a verified owned image, an allowed openly licensed alternative, or a text version where supported. Keep other affected destinations in draft.
