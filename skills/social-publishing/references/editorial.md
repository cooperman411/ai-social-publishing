# Editorial and image verification

- Start with an accurate, specific fact or useful observation rather than
  generic promotional language. Do not invent facts, statistics, quotations,
  customers, or results. Prefer current primary sources and link them.
- Adapt to each platform; do not automatically reuse the same caption.
  Facebook Pages may favor fuller context; Instagram needs strong visual
  relevance and an appropriate caption; Threads may use a concise post or a
  reply chain when supported. Observe live platform limits rather than
  depending on historical numeric limits in this document.
- The author controls brand voice, hashtags, CTA, posting frequency, and
  whether a given destination should receive the item. Do not prescribe an
  arbitrary daily posting quota or hashtag count.
- Images and video need exact source URL, rights/license, usage restrictions,
  attribution string, and a visual review. Do not assume all material from a
  public site or space agency has the same license or unrestricted commercial
  rights. Record if permission is required or unresolved.
- Do not generate another person's face, a branded product screenshot, or
  copyrighted asset and assert it is licensed. Review usage rights independently.
- Use no asset whose content cannot be verified against the caption.
- The fictional example illustrates structure, **not** a current factual claim.
