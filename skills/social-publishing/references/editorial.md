# Find and Write the Story

## Find the Story

1. Read the brief and check the calendar and recent post history for the same subject or angle.
2. Search the sources most relevant to the topic. Start with an original announcement, official record, collection entry, or other primary source. Check the event date separately from the date the source was published.
3. Record the subject, one concrete fact, the angle, and why the audience would care. Mark claims that still need verification.
4. Select stories that fit the brief and the available slots. Prefer a story with useful facts and a suitable image. Leave a slot open when the evidence is too thin.
5. Connect a story to the organization when the relationship is specific and supported. An educational story can stand on its own.

## Choose the Voice

Use the operator's writing guide, approved examples, or tone description when supplied. When there is no guide, write in plain language. Lead with the point. Use active verbs, concrete facts, and words the audience uses. Keep the same term for the same thing.

Start with one verified detail. Explain the story in a few sentences. Add a relevant business connection or one clear next action when it helps. Place the exact image credit where the reader can find it.

Adapt the post for each channel. Facebook can carry more context. For Instagram, write a caption that makes sense with the actual image. On Threads, use a short post or a supported reply chain when the story needs room. Confirm the channel's current format limits and use the operator's preferences for links and hashtags.

Before review, read the copy aloud. Cut stock openings, filler, repeated claims, hype, and engagement bait. Check every claim, number, date, and named source. Check that the photo and credit still match the final caption.
