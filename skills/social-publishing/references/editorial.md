# Find and Write the Story

## Choose a Story

Read the brief and recent post history. Search for a story that fits the audience and the available posting slots. Start with a primary source such as an original announcement, an official record, or a museum entry. Check when the event happened and when the source was published.

Write down one fact, the source, and why the story is worth sharing. If you’ve covered the subject recently, name what changed or what readers would learn from the new angle. Leave the slot open if you can’t verify the point.

## Write the Post

Use the person’s guide, sample posts, or tone description. If they haven’t supplied one, write plainly and use words the audience knows.

Start with a verified detail. Explain the story in a few sentences. Add a connection to the business when it follows from the facts. Give the reader one clear next step when the post calls for one.

Adapt the copy to the channel. A Facebook post can give more context. An Instagram caption should work with the actual photo or video. A Threads story can fit in one post or use a reply chain if Muse supports it on that account. Check the format and add the image credit where readers can find it.

Before review, read the post aloud. Cut stock openings, repeated claims, hype, and engagement bait. Check the names, dates, links, facts, and credit against their sources.
